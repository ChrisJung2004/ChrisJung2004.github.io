The 30-Day Route: A Backpacking Route Planner

Pick your kind of trip, pick a region, and get a month-long backpacking route.

CSci 3054 Web Programming · Project 2 Topic Proposal · Chris Jung

Topic Description

My website will be a backpacking route planner. The duration of the trip is set at a month and users get to choose the theme and the region of the trip. Traveling is something I have been passionate about since I was a kid and went on my first big trip to Jamaica. I fell in love with the feeling you experience when you are traveling, and it is even the reason I chose computer science as my major. I wanted to be able to work remotely and travel the world. I am also doing a 40-day backpacking trip in December after I graduate, so it is directly relevant to me right now. Traveling is a lot of fun, but planning is often the hardest part, so my website is designed to help with that and take the pressure out of planning.

There are many websites similar to mine, such as Rome2Rio, Wanderlog, or Stippl. Rome2Rio shows the logistics of getting from place to place by train, bus, flight, or ferry. Wanderlog is a full planner with a map, itinerary, and budget. Stippl offers AI tools that build your dream trip based on your description. Sites such as Lonely Planet Journeys publish curated itineraries of a set length, but they are paid, bookable packages organized by region. Where my website differs is that it offers curated, easy routes based on what type of trip you are looking for. My users don't need to already know where they want to go. All they have to do is pick what type of trip they want and which region they want to see.

App Description

Page 1: Home

The home page will have the name of my website at the top, “The 30-Day Route,” and then a pitch along the lines of “Pick your kind of trip, pick a region, and get a month-long backpacking route.” I want the background image to be something that inspires travel, so it will likely be a picture of someone summiting a mountain. I will try to find the picture on Wikimedia Commons, Unsplash, or another site like those. There will also be a sidebar that allows the user to access the other pages. Then there will be a short paragraph explaining the purpose of the website and a small section describing how the selector works, along with a simple diagram. The walkthrough of the selector will be presented as an ordered list:

  1. Pick the kind of trip you want: party, skiing, nature, or culture and food.
  2. Pick one of the four regions offered for that theme.
  3. Read the ready-made month-long route that matches those two choices.

Page 2: The Interactive Choice-Based Page

For the interactive choice-based page, my first four choices will be themes the user can pick for the trip. The options are party, skiing, nature, and culture/food. Then the next four choices will be the region you would like to go to. For the party theme, the four regions you can choose from will be Southeast Asia, Mediterranean Islands, Central European Cities, and South America. For skiing, the regions are The Alps, Nordic Countries, East Asia, and The Andes. Then for nature, the options are Nordic Countries, Patagonia, The Himalayas, and North American National Parks. Finally, for the culture/food theme, the options are Mexico & Guatemala, East Asia, Greece & Turkey, and Western Mediterranean. Some of these options overlap with each other, but the actual locations visited for each overlapping theme are completely different. That, along with which season is best for each region, will be clearly shown when allowing the user to choose a region.

Once a region is selected, a picture of the region will be displayed along with information about specific spots like restaurants, ski trails, bars and clubs, historical monuments, museums, and hiking trails. There will be enough spots and activities displayed to fill up the entire month, along with other information about places to stay. There will also be a guide on how long to stay in each location for. Every one of the sixteen routes will use this same set of information, so the same display code can be reused for any of them later in the course.

Page 3: The Static Information Page

The static page will contain historical and cultural information about the regions and the specific locations within each region. It will contain information about cultural dos and don'ts so users can make sure they are being respectful in each region. The page will also have additional spots and activities aside from the ones already listed on the interactive page, in case the user wants to change things up. This page requires no interaction from the user and does not repeat the route data from the two-step page.

Page 4: Contact Us

The contact page will be a static form until we get to the database portion of the course, so nothing submitted through it will be stored yet. The form will collect the user's name and email address so that I am able to reply to them, and a subject chosen from a short dropdown list: a question about a route, a suggested route or region, a correction such as a closed hostel or a broken link, or something else. Using a fixed list of subjects keeps messages sorted and will make a clean database column later on. The form will also ask which theme the user is most interested in, which tells me which routes are worth expanding first, and it will include a message box for the actual question or suggestion, along with an optional checkbox to be emailed when new routes are added. The site does not collect any payment or credit card information anywhere, since the application has no business value of that kind.
