Nordic Countries: Summer fjords and islands

Waterfalls, glaciers, volcanoes, and fjords, all under a sun that barely sets. You drive Iceland's Ring Road first, then go to Norway for the fjords and the Lofoten Islands. In June and July you have almost 24 hours of daylight to hike.

Route order: fly into Reykjavík and rent a car for the Ring Road, fly to Bergen, then go north to Lofoten by plane and ferry, and finish in Tromsø.
Stop Nights Highlights
Iceland's Ring Road 10 Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss waterfalls; black sand at Reynisfjara; icebergs at Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon; Mývatn's lava fields
Bergen & the fjords, Norway 8 The Flåm Railway; a boat on the narrow Nærøyfjord; the Trolltunga hike (10–12 hours round trip)
Lofoten Islands, Norway 9 The Reinebringen hike above Reine; red fishing cabins (rorbuer); midnight sun over white beaches
Tromsø, Norway 3 The Fjellheisen cable car; the Arctic Cathedral; a midnight sun hike
Total 30 4 stops

Where to stay

  • Iceland: campervans or campsites keep costs down. Guesthouses book up months ahead
  • Bergen: hostels near the Bryggen wharf
  • Lofoten: rorbuer cabins shared between friends
  • Tromsø: city hostels near the harbor

At a glance

Best time: June to August, when the midnight sun is up and the mountain roads are open.

Daily budget: About €120–200 per person per day. Camping cuts this a lot.

Getting around: A rental car or campervan in Iceland, then trains, ferries and the Hurtigruten coastal ship in Norway.

Fly in / fly out: Arrive in Reykjavík (KEF) and leave from Tromsø (TOS).
