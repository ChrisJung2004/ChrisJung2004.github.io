East Asia: Japan & Korea powder

Japan gets some of the deepest and lightest snow in the world, which skiers call "Japow." You start in Niseko, the powder capital of Hokkaido, then go to Hakuba, where the 1998 Olympics were held. Then you finish at the 2018 Olympic resorts in South Korea. Also there are hot springs and ramen after every ski day.

Route order: fly into Sapporo for Niseko, fly to Tokyo, take the shinkansen (bullet train) toward Hakuba, then fly to Seoul and take the KTX train to Pyeongchang.
Stop Nights Highlights
Niseko, Japan 10 Deep powder in Grand Hirafu; night skiing under floodlights; izakaya dinners and onsen (hot spring) soaks
Tokyo, Japan 3 A rest stop: Shibuya Crossing, Tsukiji Outer Market, and ramen in Shinjuku
Hakuba, Japan 9 Hakuba Valley's ten resorts, including Happo-one from the 1998 Olympics; a day trip to see the snow monkeys at Jigokudani
Pyeongchang, South Korea 8 Yongpyong and Alpensia from the 2018 Winter Olympics; Korean barbecue; a final day exploring Seoul
Total 30 4 stops

Where to stay

  • Niseko: shared lodges in Hirafu; cheaper rooms are in nearby Kutchan
  • Tokyo: capsule hotels or hostels in Asakusa
  • Hakuba: guesthouses in Echoland, close to bars and the shuttle
  • Pyeongchang: resort condos at Yongpyong, or stay in Seoul and day-trip by KTX

At a glance

Best time: December to March. January and February bring the deepest snow in Japan.

Daily budget: About $120–200 per person per day including lift passes. Japan is cheaper than the Alps for skiing.

Getting around: Shinkansen and local trains in Japan, a short flight to Seoul, then the KTX to Pyeongchang. Luggage delivery services let you ship your skis ahead.

Fly in / fly out: Arrive in Sapporo (CTS) and leave from Seoul (ICN).
