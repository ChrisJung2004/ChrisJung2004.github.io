Nordic Countries: Winter slopes under the northern lights

You start skiing above the Arctic Circle in Finland, then cross into Sweden and Norway for the biggest resorts in Scandinavia. The mountains are smaller than the Alps, but the lift lines are short, the snow is dry, and if you get a clear night you can see the northern lights over the runs.

Route order: fly into Kittilä for Levi, fly to Stockholm and take the train to Åre, cross into Norway by train, and finish in Oslo.
Stop Nights Highlights
Levi, Finland 9 Finland's largest ski resort, above the Arctic Circle; northern lights hunting; husky and reindeer sledding
Åre, Sweden 9 Sweden's biggest resort on the slopes of Åreskutan; lively après-ski in the village; ski-in cafés with views over the frozen lake
Hemsedal, Norway 9 Called the "Scandinavian Alps"; steep tree runs and off-piste; one of Norway's best après-ski scenes
Oslo, Norway 3 The Holmenkollen ski jump and ski museum; the Opera House roof; a floating sauna on the Oslofjord
Total 30 4 stops

Where to stay

  • Levi: self-catering cabins split between friends are cheaper than hotels
  • Åre: hostels and shared apartments in Åre village
  • Hemsedal: Skarsnuten or village apartments close to the lifts
  • Oslo: city-center hostels near Oslo Central Station

At a glance

Best time: December to April. Days are very short in December; March brings longer light and still-strong northern lights.

Daily budget: About €150–250 per person per day. Norway is the most expensive stop on the whole site.

Getting around: Domestic flights in Finland, then SJ and Vy trains across Sweden and Norway, and ski buses in each resort.

Fly in / fly out: Arrive in Kittilä (KTT) and leave from Oslo (OSL).
