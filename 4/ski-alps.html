The Alps

Four of Europe's most legendary ski towns across France, Switzerland, Austria and Italy, all connected by train. Start under Mont Blanc, ski in the shadow of the Matterhorn, earn your après-ski in the Arlberg, and finish with a lap around the Dolomites. Best season: December to April, with January through March offering the most reliable snow.

Route order: fly into Geneva, then go west to east, and fly home from Venice. Each stop is one train ride from the next.
Stop Nights Highlights
Chamonix, France 7 Aiguille du Midi cable car (3,842 m) with Mont Blanc views; the Vallée Blanche, a ~20 km off-piste glacier run (guide required); Grands Montets
Zermatt, Switzerland 7 Car-free village under the Matterhorn; Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, Europe's highest cable car station; ski across the border to Cervinia, Italy, for lunch
St. Anton, Austria 8 Ski Arlberg, about 300 km of connected runs; the Valluga summit; famous après-ski at the MooserWirt
Dolomites, Italy 8 Sella Ronda, a ~40 km ski loop around the Sella massif; Dolomiti Superski pass (~1,200 km of runs); mountain-hut (rifugio) lunches; UNESCO World Heritage site
Total 30 4 stops

Where to stay

  • Chamonix: hostel dorms in town, near the free ski buses
  • Zermatt: Zermatt Youth Hostel, the cheapest bed in an expensive town
  • St. Anton: a small guesthouse (called a Pension in Austria), or stay in nearby Pettneu for lower prices
  • Dolomites: a B&B in Ortisei or Selva in Val Gardena, on the Sella Ronda

At a glance

Best time: December to April. Avoid February if you can, because European school holidays drive up prices and crowds.

Daily budget: About €150–220 per person including lift passes, the biggest single cost at €70–100 a day. Switzerland is the most expensive stop.

Getting around: Trains between towns (SBB in Switzerland, ÖBB in Austria, Trenitalia in Italy) and free local ski buses. A Eurail pass covers every train leg.

Fly in / fly out: Arrive in Geneva (about 1 hour 15 minutes to Chamonix by shuttle) and leave from Venice (about 3 hours from the Dolomites).
