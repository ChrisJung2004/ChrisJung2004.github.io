Central European Cities

This route goes through four cities with some of the best and cheapest nightlife in Europe. Berlin has techno clubs that stay open all weekend, Prague has beer that is cheaper than water, Kraków has vodka bars in the old Jewish quarter, and Budapest has its ruin bars. Every leg is just one train ride.

Route order: fly into Berlin, then go south and east by train, and fly home from Budapest.
Stop Nights Highlights
Berlin, Germany 8 Techno clubs like Berghain and Watergate (strict door policies); RAW-Gelände; the East Side Gallery on the old Berlin Wall
Prague, Czech Republic 7 Karlovy Lázně, a five-floor club by the Charles Bridge; Czech pilsner in traditional pubs; Old Town Square
Kraków, Poland 7 Bars in the Kazimierz district; the Wieliczka Salt Mine; a respectful day visit to the Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial
Budapest, Hungary 8 Ruin bars such as Szimpla Kert; Saturday night "Sparty" at the Széchenyi Baths; a night walk along the Danube
Total 30 4 stops

Where to stay

  • Berlin: hostels in Friedrichshain or Kreuzberg, near the clubs
  • Prague: hostels in Old Town or the cheaper Žižkov neighborhood
  • Kraków: hostels in Kazimierz, the center of the nightlife
  • Budapest: party hostels in the Jewish Quarter (District VII), near the ruin bars

At a glance

Best time: Any time of year. Summer brings open-air clubs and beer gardens; winter brings Christmas markets.

Daily budget: About €60–100 per person per day. Berlin is the most expensive stop.

Getting around: Trains between every city (Deutsche Bahn, České dráhy, PKP Intercity). A Eurail pass covers them all.

Fly in / fly out: Arrive in Berlin (BER) and leave from Budapest (BUD).
